Title: Generalized Hyperrectangular Prediction Sets in Multivariate Conformal Prediction
Nabil Alami, Souhaib Ben Taieb
Subjects: Machine Learning (stat.ML); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

In multivariate conformal prediction, many nonconformity scores yield prediction sets with complex geometry (e.g., ellipsoids or density level sets) that are difficult to interpret and visualize. Hyperrectangles offer a practical alternative by providing one interval per output. We extend this notion to Cartesian products of arbitrary one-dimensional sets, which we call generalized hyperrectangles. We introduce a rectangularization framework that turns any continuous multivariate nonconformity score, or conditional density, into one yielding generalized hyperrectangular prediction sets with finite-sample coverage. We establish key geometric properties of our rectangularization framework and provide useful intuitions on generalized hyperrectangles. We further establish a representation theorem showing that a broad class of hyperrectangular conformal methods based on max-aggregated coordinate-wise scores can be interpreted as rectangularizations of implicit conditional densities. Through synthetic and real-world experiments, we quantify the efficiency cost of rectangularization, while highlighting its benefits, especially for practical applications of uncertainty quantification.

Title: A Unified Framework for Training-Dataset Aggregation and Exact Machine Unlearning via Gibbs Probability Measures
Yaiza Bermudez, Samir M. Perlaza, Iñaki Esnaola
Comments: Submitted to the IEEE Transaction on Information Theory
Subjects: Machine Learning (stat.ML); Information Theory (cs.IT); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

In this paper, three operations on Gibbs probability measures are studied: renormalization, normalized log-linear combination, and nesting. The resulting measures are shown to be Gibbs probability measures with explicit expressions for their objective functions, reference measures, and regularization factors. These measures are further characterized as unique solutions to optimization problems involving linear combinations of the original objective functions, subject to either relative entropy regularization or relative entropy constraints. When the reference measures are probability measures, geometric interpretations of these three operations are established involving Pythagorean identities and exponential geodesics.A central result is the equivalence between nesting and normalized log-linear combinations of Gibbs probability measures. These results are then applied to supervised machine learning with Gibbs algorithms, which are represented by Gibbs probability measures. In this context, either through nesting or log-linear combinations, Gibbs algorithms trained on local datasets are used to form new Gibbs algorithms trained upon the aggregation of all local training datasets. Interestingly, the parameters of such operations, determine the weights of each training data point on the aggregated dataset. %Operations on local Gibbs probability measures are shown to control the contributions of training data. This observation is leveraged for achieving: (a) exact machine unlearning in centralized, decentralized, and federated learning systems; and (b) centralized-equivalent performance, in the sense of producing algorithms trained on the aggregation of all local datasets in decentralized and federated learning .

Title: Near-Minimax Sample Complexity for Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning under Generative Models
Yue Wang
Comments: Preprint, work in progress
Subjects: Machine Learning (stat.ML); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

In multi-objective reinforcement learning, preferences may be specified only after data collection, requiring one dataset to support a continuum of reward trade-offs. We study finite discounted Markov decision processes with unknown transitions, $S$ states, $A$ actions, and $m$ known reward functions taking values in $[0,1]$, given access to a generative model. The learner must provide an $\varepsilon$-optimal policy for every convex combination of these rewards without further sampling, with a guarantee that holds simultaneously over all preferences and initial states. Writing $H=(1-\gamma)^{-1}$, we establish the constant-confidence minimax sample complexity $\widetilde\Theta\!\left(
\frac{SAH^3}{\varepsilon^2}\min\{S,\log(em)\} \right)$, where the suppressed factors are logarithmic in $S,A,H,\varepsilon^{-1}$ and the dependence on $m$ is explicit. Thus, up to these logarithmic factors, the worst-case statistical cost of supporting all preferences grows logarithmically with the number of objectives and saturates at the number of states. The upper bound uses uniform state--action sampling and empirical planning, with reward rounding in the logarithmic-objective regime. Its analysis chains optimal-value increments over covers of the reward hull. A one-sided Bellman variance bound controls signed value differences under a second transition kernel, preserving the $H^3\varepsilon^{-2}$ dependence. A complementary uniform guarantee for all bounded rewards yields the state-dimension saturation. We establish lower bounds matching up to logarithmic factors through a rare-exit construction with subset rewards and action encoding. These bounds apply to adaptive simulator queries and arbitrary randomized history-dependent output policies, while the upper bounds are attained by deterministic stationary policies.

Title: Generative Modeling for Cascading Extremes via Autoregressive Transformers
Miguel de Carvalho, Myung Won Lee, Jordan Richards, Sotirios Sabanis
Comments: 28 pages, 10 figures
Subjects: Machine Learning (stat.ML); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

Extreme events often occur sequentially, from earthquake-tsunami cascades to successive extreme losses in financial markets. We introduce an extremal token representation that encodes multivariate observations as a categorical sequence. A quiet token records the absence of threshold exceedances; otherwise, the token identifies the exceeding component with the largest marginal probability-scale score. The representation is invariant to componentwise strictly increasing transformations. It preserves the identity and temporal ordering of dominant exceedances but discards their magnitudes and the identities of nondominant simultaneous exceedances. A decoder-only transformer estimates the history-dependent next-token kernel on this representation, and recursive sampling generates future token sequences from the induced joint predictive law. We amortize our generative model by pretraining across simulated process families with varying parameters and dependence persistence. Next-token predictions are evaluated against exact conditional probabilities for held-out categorical processes and particle filter references for heavy-tailed vector autoregressive processes. Applied to ten-second returns for five semiconductor stocks, the pretrained model supports zero-shot transfer, while a few epochs of adaptation improve agreement between observed and generated token frequencies and transition patterns. These results support task-specific tokenization and simulation-based pretraining for modeling which dimensions dominate multivariate extremes.

Title: Risk-Sensitive Non-Parametric and Kernel Bandits
Devdan Dey, Sujoy Bhore, Avishek Ghosh
Comments: 46 pages, 6 figures
Subjects: Machine Learning (stat.ML); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

We study non-parametric bandits in which arms are evaluated using Cumulative Prospect Theory (CPT) rather than expected reward. We consider continuous action spaces with arm-dependent noise and propose Non-Parametric CPT Bandits ($\mathtt{NPCB}$), an optimistic algorithm that decouples mean estimation from CPT-based decision-making. We prove CPT regret of order $\widetilde{\mathcal O}\bigl(\sqrt{T\,\dim_E(\mathcal F,T^{-2})\,\log N(\mathcal F,T^{-2},\|\cdot\|_\infty)}\bigr)$, where $\dim_E$ is the Eluder dimension of the function class $\mathcal F$, $T$ is the time horizon, and $N$ is its covering number in the sup norm. This matches the order-wise regret of classical non-parametric bandits, showing that CPT risk sensitivity incurs no additional order-wise regret cost. We extend $\mathtt{NPCB}$ to unknown noise CDFs, retaining the known-CDF mean-learning term and adding a CDF-learning cost governed by a distributional Eluder dimension. We also study Kernel-CPT bandits and obtain information-gain-based regret bounds for a broad range of kernels, matching the corresponding risk-neutral kernel rates up to logarithmic factors.

Title: Tight Lower Bounds under Matched High-Order Hölder Smoothness and Uniform Convexity
Cedar Site Bai
Subjects: Optimization and Control (math.OC); Machine Learning (cs.LG); Machine Learning (stat.ML)

In this paper, we provide tight lower bounds for minimizing high-order Hölder-smooth and uniformly convex functions when their orders match. Specifically, we consider functions whose $p^{th}$-order derivatives are Hölder continuous with degree $\nu$ and parameter $H$, and that are uniformly convex with degree $q$ and parameter $\sigma$. When these orders match, namely, $q = p + \nu$, we show that any deterministic algorithm with access to derivatives up to order $p$ requires $\Omega\left(\left(\frac{H}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{2}{3(p+\nu)-2}}\log\frac{\Delta_0}{\epsilon}\right)$ oracle calls to reduce an initial optimality gap of $\Delta_0$ to $\epsilon$. This matches the corresponding upper bound and establishes a higher-order uniformly convex counterpart to Nesterov's classical first-order lower bound for strongly convex functions. Rather than directly generalizing Nesterov's classical quadratic-chain construction, we develop a multiscale phase-wise construction that preserves previous oracle responses while maintaining global Hölder smoothness and uniform convexity. Our result resolves the matched case left open by existing lower bounds for the two asymmetric regimes.

Support vector machines (SVMs) are fundamentally grounded in geometric margin maximisation and currently lack a mainstream probabilistic interpretation. In this paper, we bridge this gap by proving that the dual objectives of both support vector classification and support vector regression are exact, constrained Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence minimisation tasks between two Gaussian measures. Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG); Computation (stat.CO); Machine Learning (stat.ML)

Variational approaches to learning transport maps for multimodal sampling can suffer from mode collapse. One way to mitigate this problem is to learn transport along a prescribed path of intermediate distributions defined by geometric annealing.
